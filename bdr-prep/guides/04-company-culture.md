# PostHog Company Culture Guide

Understanding PostHog's culture is critical for success. They explicitly state that culture fit is MORE important than skills—skills can be taught, but poor culture fit affects the entire team.

## Core Identity

### All-Remote Company

PostHog's team is **100% remote** and distributed across **20+ countries**.

Benefits they emphasize:
- Hire from a global talent pool
- Thoughtful, intentional written communication
- Space for uninterrupted work
- Judge performance on outcomes, not hours in office

### Everyone Codes

Almost everyone at PostHog is a developer—over 70% of the team are engineers. This includes:
- Marketing team members
- Sales team members (you!)
- Operations team members

This is deliberate. It ensures everyone can contribute technically and understand the product deeply.

### Transparency as Default

PostHog defaults to transparency with **everything**:
- Code is open source
- Handbook is public
- Roadmap is public
- Compensation practices are public
- Strategy is public
- Investor information is public

Why? It builds trust with their technical audience and attracts like-minded talent.

## Core Values

### 1. We Are Open

**Externally:**
- Code is open source
- Handbook available to everyone
- Share learnings publicly
- Transparent pricing

**Internally:**
- Share feedback directly with the person
- Team changes happen in public Slack channels
- Access to detailed financial information
- Board slides available to everyone

### 2. We Ship Fast

- Bias for action over analysis
- "Why not now?" mentality
- Learn by doing, not hypothesizing
- If debating something, just try it

### 3. We Don't Tiptoe

- Direct communication
- Say what you mean
- No passive-aggressive behavior
- Assume positive intent

### 4. We Are Self-Starters

- Don't need consensus to do things
- Focus energy on what's most valuable
- Take ownership of making it happen
- Don't wait for permission

### 5. We Build for the Long Term

- Focus on sustainable growth
- Don't sacrifice quality for speed
- Think about compound effects
- Build trust over time

## Communication Style

### Async-First

Asynchronous communication is the starting point at PostHog.

**What this means:**
- Don't expect immediate responses
- Write things down clearly
- Most communication is on GitHub (public issues and PRs)
- Use Slack for discussions but document decisions

**Why async:**
- Distributed team across time zones
- Allows deep work without interruptions
- Creates a written record
- Reduces meeting overhead

### Public by Default

Communication happens openly:
- GitHub issues and PRs are public
- Most Slack channels are public
- Decisions are documented for everyone

### Written Communication

Because they're async-first, writing well is essential:
- Be clear and concise
- Provide context
- Structure your thoughts
- Make it scannable

## Small Teams Structure

### How It Works

PostHog is organized into small teams, typically capped at **6 members**.

Each small team:
- Has complete control over their product
- Makes their own decisions
- Controls their own roadmap
- Handles their own marketing and pricing

### Why Small Teams?

Benefits they've found:
- Speed through delegated decision-making
- Clear ownership and accountability
- Less coordination overhead
- More autonomy for team members

### Implications for You

As Founding BDR, you'll likely be a small team (maybe even a team of one initially). This means:
- High autonomy
- High ownership
- You make decisions
- You move fast

## Work Philosophy

### PRs Over Issues

PostHog tries to create a culture of **submitting pull requests over opening issues**.

What this means:
- If you find a bug you can fix, just fix it
- If you want to build something you can handle, do it
- Don't just open a ticket for someone else
- Bias toward action

### Autonomy and Ownership

You don't need consensus to do things. The expectation is:
- Identify what's most valuable
- Take ownership
- Make it happen
- Learn from results

### Bias for Action ("Why not now?")

PostHog has learned that:
- The clearest lessons come from doing
- Not from hypothesizing
- If you're debating, try it
- Action resolves uncertainty

## What They Look For in Hires

### Culture Fit is #1

PostHog explicitly states:

> "We need to be particularly sensitive to culture at this stage. We can handle someone underperforming much better than someone who is a poor culture fit due to the impact on the broader team."

This means culture fit matters more than:
- Past experience
- Technical skills
- Credentials

### Specific Qualities

1. **Self-starters** who don't need management
2. **Comfortable with ambiguity** and uncertainty
3. **Strong written communicators**
4. **Direct and honest** in feedback
5. **Action-oriented** over analysis-paralysis
6. **Ownership mentality** over task completion

### Many Team Members Are Former Founders

PostHog attracts people who've started their own companies. Why?

> "There they can focus on building great products without the distraction of running payroll, fundraising, etc - while still enjoying huge upside potential."

If you have founder experience or mentality, emphasize it.

Don't wait for everyone to agree:
- Make decisions

- Public handbook

- No information silos

## How to Demonstrate Culture Fit

### In Your Application

1. **Show self-starting behavior**
   - What have you built or done without being asked?
   - How do you identify what to work on?

2. **Demonstrate action orientation**
   - Give examples of just trying things
   - Show how you learn by doing

3. **Prove you can work async**
   - Can you communicate clearly in writing?
   - Do you need constant supervision?

4. **Be direct and honest**
   - Don't hedge or over-qualify
   - Say what you mean

### In Interviews

1. **Ask thoughtful questions** about how they work
2. **Share specific examples** of ownership
3. **Be honest** about what you don't know
4. **Show genuine interest** in their culture

### Red Flags to Avoid

- Needing constant guidance
- Waiting for permission
- Being indirect or political
- Over-relying on process
- Prioritizing credentials over outcomes

## Self-Check: Are You a Culture Fit?

Ask yourself honestly:

- [ ] Can I work effectively without daily supervision?
- [ ] Am I comfortable making decisions without full information?
- [ ] Do I communicate clearly in writing?
- [ ] Can I give and receive direct feedback?
- [ ] Do I take ownership of outcomes, not just tasks?
- [ ] Am I motivated by the work itself, not external structure?
- [ ] Can I work across time zones asynchronously?
- [ ] Am I honest about what I don't know?

If you answered "no" to several of these, PostHog might not be the right fit—and that's okay. Better to know now than to struggle later.
