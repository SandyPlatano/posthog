# PostHog Culture Call - Quick Study Guide

**For your recruiter culture call - all the essentials in one place**

---

## THE 5 CORE VALUES

### 1. We Are Open 🔓
**Externally:**
- Fully open source codebase
- Public handbook, roadmap, pricing
- Share learnings and failures publicly
- Transparent about strategy and even investor info

**Internally:**
- Direct feedback culture
- Public Slack channels for all team changes
- Everyone has access to financial data and board slides
- No information silos

**Your talking point:** "I appreciate the radical transparency - having the handbook public shows confidence and builds trust with technical audiences."

---

### 2. We Ship Fast 🚀
- **Bias for action** over endless analysis
- **"Why not now?"** mentality
- Learn by doing, not hypothesizing
- If you're debating something, just try it and learn from results

**Your talking point:** "I'm action-oriented - I'd rather test an outbound campaign idea quickly and iterate than spend weeks perfecting it first."

---

### 3. We Don't Tiptoe 🗣️
- Direct, honest communication
- Say what you mean clearly
- No passive-aggressive behavior
- No corporate speak or politics
- Assume positive intent from others

**Your talking point:** "I value direct feedback - I'd rather hear honest input early than discover issues later."

---

### 4. We Are Self-Starters 💪
- Don't need consensus to do things
- Focus energy on what's most valuable
- Take ownership of making it happen
- Don't wait for permission or approval

**Your talking point:** "I thrive with autonomy - I naturally identify what needs to be done and take ownership without needing hand-holding."

---

### 5. We Build for the Long Term 🌱
- Focus on sustainable growth over quick wins
- Don't sacrifice quality for speed
- Think about compound effects
- Build trust with customers over time

**Your talking point:** "I'm excited about building the outbound motion from scratch - focusing on sustainable systems rather than burning through leads for quick numbers."

---

## KEY CULTURE CHARACTERISTICS

### 100% Remote & Distributed
- Team across 20+ countries
- Async-first communication
- Judge on outcomes, not office time
- Written communication is critical

### Everyone Codes (70%+ Engineers)
- Even marketing and sales team members code
- Ensures deep product understanding
- You'll be expected to implement PostHog yourself

### Small Teams (Max 6 People)
- Complete autonomy and ownership
- Control your own roadmap
- Make your own decisions
- As Founding BDR, you might be a team of one initially

---

## WHAT POSTHOG LOOKS FOR

### Culture Fit > Skills
**This is explicitly stated:** Culture fit matters MORE than past experience or technical skills. Skills can be taught, but poor culture fit affects the entire team.

### Specific Qualities They Want:
1. **Self-starters** who don't need management
2. **Comfortable with ambiguity** and uncertainty
3. **Strong written communicators** (async-first requires this)
4. **Direct and honest** with feedback
5. **Action-oriented** over analysis-paralysis
6. **Ownership mentality** over just completing tasks

### Many Team Members Are Former Founders
They attract people with founder mentality because they can focus on building without the distraction of running payroll, fundraising, etc., while still getting huge upside.

**If you have entrepreneurial experience, emphasize it.**

## WORK PHILOSOPHY

### PRs Over Issues
- If you can fix something, fix it
- Don't just open tickets for others
- Bias toward action
- Take ownership

### Autonomy and Ownership
- Don't need consensus
- Identify what's valuable
- Make it happen
- Learn from results

### Async-First Communication
- Don't expect immediate responses
- Write clearly with context
- Most communication on GitHub (public)
- Document decisions

---

## ANTI-PATTERNS TO AVOID

**They've explicitly "optimized against" typical corporate structures:**
- No unnecessary hierarchy
- No excessive process
- No consensus-driven decision making
- No knowledge hoarding
- No waiting for approval
- No coordination overhead

**Red flags in your behavior:**
- Needing constant guidance
- Waiting for permission
- Being indirect or political
- Over-relying on process
- Prioritizing credentials over outcomes

## QUESTIONS YOU COULD ASK

These show you understand the culture:

1. **"How do you maintain culture fit as you scale globally?"**
   - Shows you understand the remote/distributed challenge

2. **"What does 'shipping fast' look like day-to-day for the sales team?"**
   - Shows you're thinking about applying values to your role

3. **"Can you share an example of when someone demonstrated 'we don't tiptoe'?"**
   - Shows genuine interest in how values play out

4. **"As a founding BDR, what level of autonomy should I expect in building the outbound motion?"**
   - Shows you understand the self-starter expectation

5. **"How does the sales team contribute technically? Should I expect to code regularly?"**
   - Shows you've researched the "everyone codes" aspect

## KEY TALKING POINTS FOR YOUR CALL

### When They Ask "Why PostHog?"
"I'm drawn to the culture of autonomy and ownership. The fact that culture fit is prioritized over skills shows you're building for the long term. I thrive in environments where I can identify what's valuable and make it happen without waiting for permission."

### When They Ask About Remote Work
"I'm excited about async-first communication. I've found that writing things down clearly not only helps distributed teams but creates better documentation and more thoughtful decisions."

### When They Ask About Your Work Style
"I'm very much a self-starter. I naturally take ownership and bias toward action. I'd rather try something, learn quickly, and iterate than spend weeks planning. That aligns well with 'why not now?'"

### When They Ask About Feedback
"I value direct communication. I'd rather hear honest feedback early so I can adjust quickly. And I'll be direct with others too - no tiptoeing."

## REMEMBER

**Most Important:** PostHog explicitly states that **culture fit is MORE important than skills**. They can teach you the technical aspects, but they can't teach you to be a self-starter or to communicate directly.

**Be authentic.** If you naturally align with these values, let it show. If you don't, PostHog might not be the right fit - and that's okay.

**Be specific.** Share concrete examples from your experience that demonstrate these values in action.

**Ask questions.** Show genuine curiosity about how they operationalize these values day-to-day.
