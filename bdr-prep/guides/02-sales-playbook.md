# PostHog Sales Playbook

This guide covers PostHog's unique approach to sales. Read this carefully - it's fundamentally different from traditional B2B SaaS sales.

## The PostHog Sales Philosophy

### Core Principle: Make Paying Customers Successful

PostHog's primary focus is on **making paying customers successful**, not forcing sales through.

This means:
- **No BS sales-y talk** - Be direct, open, and honest
- **Share as much as possible publicly** - Don't hide behind mandatory demos
- **Be honest when you don't know** - Or when PostHog isn't the right solution
- **Qualify out** - Don't waste time on bad fits

### What PostHog DOESN'T Want

Direct quote from their hiring docs:

> "We don't want to end up taking cold leads from BDRs so we can run MEDPICC from our car phone while promising 50% discounts if they sign before the next full moon."

They explicitly warn against:
- Traditional enterprise sales tactics
- High-pressure closing techniques
- Over-discounting to hit numbers
- Complex sales methodologies executed poorly
- Executing your previous company's playbook

## The Sales Model

### Primarily Inbound, Experimenting Outbound

PostHog has historically been completely inbound. Their CEO states: "PostHog doesn't do any outbound sales at all. We're a completely inbound business."

As Founding BDR, you're helping them experiment with outbound while maintaining their values.

### Revenue Target: $20k+ Annual Customers

The sales team focuses on customers who are paying or could pay **$20k+/year**.

Why this threshold?
- Below $20k: Self-serve handles these customers efficiently
- Above $20k: Worth the sales team's time and customization

## Lead Qualification Process

### Initial Qualification (Before Scheduling a Call)

When assigned a lead, qualify by asking:

1. **Company size and revenue?**
   - Target: 15-500 employees
   - Revenue: $100k+/month

2. **Have they raised funding?**
   - Are they likely to pay >$20k?
   - Look for Series B+ or top-tier investors

3. **Role of the contact?**
   - Engineering roles score highest (6 points)
   - Leadership/Product roles (3 points each)

4. **Are engineers involved?**
   - Engineers must be part of the buying process
   - If not, getting them involved is step one

### PostHog's Unique Approach to Friction

> "Most companies add friction by making customers jump on a call first to qualify them. PostHog doesn't do this when they are confident that product engineers are, or will become, involved in the sale."

This means:
- Don't force unnecessary calls
- Let qualified prospects self-serve when possible
- Add value in every interaction

## The Sales Conversation

### Be Technical, Not Sales-y

PostHog wants someone who:
- Can carry a sales conversation
- Has technical chops to talk to engineers
- Can get their hands dirty
- Can own technical problems

### Communication Style

**Do:**
- Be direct and specific
- Share information openly
- Admit uncertainty
- Talk like an engineer to engineers
- Focus on solving their actual problem

**Don't:**
- Use sales jargon
- Be vague or evasive
- Overpromise
- Push for unnecessary meetings
- Treat technical people like non-technical buyers

### Handling Technical Questions

You are expected to answer technical questions yourself. This includes:
- How to implement PostHog
- Which SDK to use
- How features work
- Integration questions
- Basic debugging

If you don't know: "I don't know, but let me find out" is always acceptable.

## Pricing Conversations

### Transparent Pricing Philosophy

PostHog has moved to transparent, self-serve pricing for ALL plans, including Enterprise.

> "You can now sign up for an Enterprise license yourself in a fully automated manner, so all plans are now transparently-priced and self-serve."

This means:
- Don't hide pricing
- Don't require a call to see pricing
- Don't use pricing as leverage

### Key Price Points and Discounts

**Free Tier (Generous)**
- 1 million events/month
- 5,000 session recordings
- 1 million feature flag requests
- 100,000 exceptions
- 1,500 survey responses

**Paid Usage**
- Usage-based pricing after free tier
- Enterprise add-on: $2,000/month

**Discount Structure**

| Annual Spend | Discount |
|--------------|----------|
| >$20k prepaid | 20% |
| >$60k | Additional 5% |
| >$100k | Additional 15% |
| Multi-year | Additional 5% per year |

### Contract Customization Rules

- **<$20k**: No customization, must use standard terms
- **$20k**: Minor clarifying edits only
- **>$20k**: Higher amounts required for significant changes

Example from handbook:
> "A customer committing to spend just $20k USD annually should not expect significant deviations from PostHog's standard terms."

## Customer Types and Segments

### Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)

**Company Characteristics:**
- B2B startups
- Series B to IPO
- 15-500 employees
- $100k+/month revenue
- Top-tier investors
- Engineering-led decision making

**Target Persona:**
- High-performing product engineers
- Full-stack, frontend-skewed
- Building high-craft products
- At high-growth startups

### Why This ICP?

PostHog's reasoning:

> "We only get word-of-mouth growth by doing a remarkable job for a specific kind of user, not an average one for lots of people. We develop a strong feedback loop: by building for the best product engineers, we'll attract more of the best product engineers and the best companies."

### Engineers Drive Word-of-Mouth

> "Engineers have always been community-centric, and they regularly discuss and recommend tools to each other in engineering circles, which translates into referrals, broader adoption, and brand trust."

## Outbound Strategy (Your Focus)

### The Experiment

PostHog is running outbound experiments for the first time. As Founding BDR, you'll help build this motion.

### Keys to Success

1. **Stay true to PostHog values**
   - No spam
   - Provide actual value
   - Be authentic

2. **Target ICP precisely**
   - Use the scoring criteria
   - Focus on engineering-led companies
   - Look for product-market fit signals

3. **Lead with technical value**
   - Share relevant content
   - Offer genuine help
   - Don't pitch immediately

4. **Measure and iterate**
   - This is an experiment
   - Track what works
   - Be willing to try new approaches

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid

- Mass cold emails
- LinkedIn automation spam
- Fake personalization
- Aggressive follow-ups
- Promising things you can't deliver
- Treating it like traditional BDR work

## Common Scenarios

### Scenario 1: Engineer Wants to Try PostHog

**Action:** Let them self-serve. Offer to help if they get stuck.

Don't force a meeting. The product sells itself to technical users.

### Scenario 2: Non-Technical Buyer Reaching Out

**Action:** Ask who their engineering team is and involve them.

PostHog is built for engineers. If engineers aren't involved, the deal likely won't work.

### Scenario 3: Company Needs Enterprise Features

**Action:** Point them to self-serve Enterprise sign-up.

No "talk to sales" wall. They can sign up themselves for $2k/month.

### Scenario 4: Prospect Asks for Big Discount

**Action:** Explain the standard discount structure transparently.

Don't negotiate outside the published discounts without good reason.

### Scenario 5: Competitor Question

**Action:** Be honest about tradeoffs.

If PostHog isn't the right fit, say so. Trust builds long-term relationships.
